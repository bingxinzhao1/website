Bingxin Zhao

I am an associate professor in Statistics and Data Science at the Wharton School of the University of Pennsylvania, with a secondary appointment in the Department of Medicine at the Perelman School of Medicine.

My research develops statistical and AI methods for science and medicine, with a focus on integrating multimodal data, such as imaging, genomics, and knowledge graphs, to enable discovery and translation. I’m also working on unifying statistical rigor, software engineering, and agentic AI to build practical systems for scientific and medical research.

Research

My work mainly spans across four directions:

  • Genetics of the human brain: White matter microstructure (Science 2021), Functional networks (Nature Genetics 2022), Regional brain volumes (Nature Genetics 2019), White matter and mental health traits (Molecular Psychiatry 2021), Transcriptome-wide associations of brain structures (Nature Communications 2021)
  • Inter-organ connections: Heart-brain connections (Science 2023), Multi-organ imaging genetics (Nature Reviews Neurology 2026), Multi-organ Mendelian randomization (Nature Biomedical Engineering 2026), Rare coding variants across organs (Nature Communications 2026), Plasma proteomics and organ imaging (Nature Communications 2026), Eye-brain connections (Nature Communications 2024)
  • Statistical methods and theory with biomedical applications: LD score regression (Annals of Statistics 2025), Block-wise and reference panel-based estimators (Annals of Statistics 2024), Trans-ancestry genetic correlation (JASA 2024), Genetic correlation estimation (JASA 2022), Cross-trait prediction accuracy (Biometrics 2023), Polygenic risk scores (Biometrics 2022)
  • AI, agentic platforms, and cloud computing: AI co-scientists (Nature Genetics 2026), Self-rankings (Nature Computational Science 2026), BIGA (JASA 2026), PennPRS (Nature Methods, accepted)

Platforms for the research community

We have contributed to streamline complex data analysis into publicly accessible cloud-based computing platforms by integrating statistics and software engineering, to enhance accessibility to statistical applications and reduce computational resource disparities for the global scientific research community.

PennPRS
Centralized cloud computing platform for efficient polygenic risk score model training in precision medicine.

BIGA
